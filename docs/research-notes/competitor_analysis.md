## 1. Curriculum structure and sound order: what is taught first, and when do digraphs, blends, long vowels, alternative spellings and multisyllabic words appear?

### Takeaway
All the programmes researched use systematic synthetic phonics with a small, high-utility set of first letters. The first letters vary, though: s-a-t-p-i-n (TYMTR, Jolly, Little Wandle), m-a-s-d-t (RWI) and m-a-t-s-i-f-d-r (Duolingo ABC). After that the UK programmes follow roughly the same order: single letters, then consonant digraphs, then vowel digraphs/trigraphs (long vowels taught as digraphs), then adjacent consonants (blends), then alternative spellings in Year 1. US programmes (UFLI, Duolingo ABC) teach blends before long vowels and introduce long vowels through VCe (silent-e) first, then vowel teams. They also reach multisyllabic words and affixes earlier and in more depth.

### Inferences
- [INF] Consensus starting set across programmes: s, a, t, p, i, n, m, d (all in the first ~8 of TYMTR, Jolly, Little Wandle and RWI). For Japanese EFL learners, continuous sounds (m, s, f, n) are easier to blend audibly than stops (t, p, d, k). Japanese speakers also tend to add vowels after consonants ("tu", "pu"). Both points favour a Duolingo-like front-loading of continuants, or at least explicit "pure sound" modelling.
- [INF] UK sequence: digraphs → long vowels as digraphs → blends/clusters → alternative spellings. US sequence: digraphs → blends → VCe → vowel teams → r-controlled → affixes. Both reach decodable sentences after roughly 20–30 GPCs.
- [INF] Multisyllabic words: UFLI (lesson ~54) and Duolingo (L6) introduce them explicitly. UK phonics programmes leave them mostly to Year 1–2 (Phase 5 and beyond), and TYMTR does not visibly cover them.

## 2. Teaching methodology, lesson routines, mastery, review, mistakes, skipping and repetition

### Takeaway
Teacher-led programmes (UFLI, RWI, Jolly, Little Wandle) build mastery into a fixed daily routine: review, then new concept, then apply in words, then apply in connected text. Apps mostly gate progress linearly and rarely let children skip. Duolingo ABC, according to a reviewer, makes every child start at the beginning and gives "little learning support" for strugglers. Public documentation of in-app mastery thresholds and error handling is thin for every app.

### Cited Findings
- [PD] UFLI eight-step routine: 1 Phonemic Awareness, 2 Visual Drill, 3 Auditory Drill, 4 Blending Drill, 5 New Concept, 6 Word Work, 7 Irregular Words, 8 Connected Text. Steps 1–4 warm up and review, step 5 introduces the new concept with guided reading/spelling, and steps 6–8 apply it at word and text level — [UFLI Foundations](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations)
- [PD] The UFLI Toolbox includes lesson slide decks and decodable passages. The manual is sold via Ventris Learning, with free downloadable resources — [UFLI Foundations](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations); [UFLI Foundations materials](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations-materials/)
- [PD] RWI "Fred Talk": a toy frog who can only say sounds ("c-a-t"), and the child blends the word. Each storybook is read three times: for accuracy, for fluency, and in a "storyteller voice" — [Oxford Owl](https://home.oxfordowl.co.uk/reading/reading-schemes-oxford-levels/read-write-inc-phonics-guide/)
- [PD] Jolly Phonics: multisensory action per sound, one sound per day, five skills including letter formation — [Jolly guide PDF](https://www.lakemills.k12.wi.us/lmes/parent_resources/Curriculum/Jolly%20Phonics%20Parent%20Teacher%20Guide.pdf)
- [PD] Duolingo ABC (Common Sense Education review): "each new game reviews earlier material"; lessons follow a logical order; children can see their place on the path. Weaknesses: every child starts at the beginning, teachers cannot reorder or pick a start point, "little explicit instruction", "little learning support for kids who are struggling", single profile — [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/app/duolingo-abc-learn-to-read)
- [PD] TYMTR: activities and characters recur within each game. Game 3 explicitly says children "need to be fully prepared" (it is entry-gated by readiness, not by an in-game test) — [Horn Book](https://hbook.com/story/teach-your-monster-to-read-app-review); [TYMTR help](https://help.teachyourmonster.org/en/articles/5458453-what-does-each-game-cover)
- [PD] Reading Eggs: lessons sit on maps, and Map 13 lessons end with a Skills Quiz plus progress report — [Reading Eggs overview](https://readingeggs.com/about/lesson-overview/reading/)

### Inferences
- [INF] UFLI's routine maps directly onto an app lesson template: (1) oral phonemic warm-up, (2) quick GPC flash review (see grapheme → say/choose sound), (3) hear sound → pick grapheme, (4) blending drill with cumulative GPCs, (5) new GPC intro, (6) build/read words, (7) one or two heart/tricky words, (8) decodable sentence or passage. This is the strongest borrowable skeleton.
- [INF] RWI's "read the same book three times (accuracy → fluency → expression)" is a cheap, evidence-aligned repetition mechanic an app can copy, with optional recording on the third read.
- [INF] Recognition vs real reading: many app activities (pick the letter that makes /s/, pop the bubble with "cat") test *recognition* among 2–4 options, where a child can succeed by elimination or guessing. Only blending-to-produce (saying the word, or building it from tiles) and reading connected text check *real decoding*. Common Sense's note that Duolingo ABC uses speech recognition for reading aloud is the closest documented attempt to check production — [Common Sense](https://www.commonsense.org/education/app/duolingo-abc-learn-to-read).
- [INF] For Mark's EFL context, a "test-out / placement" option is a clear gap to fill, given Duolingo's documented one-size start point.

### Gaps
- No public documentation found of the mastery thresholds, error-retry rules or spaced-review algorithms in TYMTR, Duolingo ABC, Reading Eggs or Nessy.
- Whether TYMTR or Duolingo ABC repeat failed items adaptively: [UNK].

## 3. Reading development: when words, sentences, connected text, decodable books, fluency and comprehension appear

### Takeaway
UK programmes move from CVC words (after ~6–12 GPCs) to sentences and ditties after Set 1 / Phase 2–3, to fully decodable colour-banded books, and only then to comprehension. TYMTR mirrors this: CVC in Game 1, sentences in Game 2, little books and comprehension in Game 3. Duolingo ABC and Reading Eggs bring in narrated stories and sight words from the start. Duolingo's 2nd-grade stories are described as "leveled" rather than decodable, which is a risk flag.

### Cited Findings
- [PD] TYMTR: CVC blending in Game 1; sentence reading and comprehension in Game 2; "magical little books" and reading for meaning in Game 3; separate free book app — [TYMTR help](https://help.teachyourmonster.org/en/articles/5458453-what-does-each-game-cover)
- [PD] RWI: Set 2 readers start Red Ditty → Green → Purple books; Set 3: Pink → Orange → Yellow; all sets: Blue → Grey — [Oxford Owl](https://home.oxfordowl.co.uk/reading/reading-schemes-oxford-levels/read-write-inc-phonics-guide/)
- [PD] Duolingo ABC: Pre-K/K has narrated interactive stories highlighting target letters/words; Grade 1 has character-driven stories and stories featuring target patterns; Grade 2 has "leveled skill-building stories" with predicting, sequencing, monitoring and vocabulary inference; L9 targets fluency; there are mini-games for spelling and word-reading speed. The document has no distinct sentence-level stage — [Duolingo ABC Scope & Sequence](https://lit-lessons-cdn.duolingo.com/resources/duolingo_abc_scope_and_sequence_english.pdf)
- [PD] UFLI: every lesson ends in connected text (step 8), and the Toolbox has decodable passages — [UFLI](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations)
- [PD] Reading Eggs: first Sam the Ant stories in Maps 1–4; ~40 books in each of Maps 5–8 and 9–12, some non-fiction — [Reading Eggs](https://readingeggs.com/about/lesson-overview/reading/)
- [PD] Little Wandle: children are expected to read Phase 4 Set 2 books by the end of Y1 Autumn 1 — [Little Wandle progression doc](https://files.schudio.com/sholing-infant-school/files/documents/Little-Wandle-and-Spelling-progression-2022-with-reading.276686470.pdf)

### Inferences
- [INF] Whether Duolingo ABC's and Reading Eggs' stories are strictly decodable against taught GPCs is not documented. "Leveled" suggests they are not fully decodable. A new app should guarantee that every text uses only taught GPCs plus taught tricky words, and could show a decodability check.
- [INF] For Japanese EFL children, comprehension in early texts is limited by oral vocabulary, not just decoding. Word and sentence content should come from words they already know orally (e.g. Let's Try / New Horizon vocab, per Mark's curriculum) and be supported with pictures. None of the competitors design for this; all assume native-speaker oral vocabulary.

### Gaps
- Exact point (GPC count) where Duolingo ABC first shows connected text the child must decode independently.
- Whether TYMTR's "little books" are decodable to the GPCs taught.

## 4. App experience: activities, rewards, friction and educational weaknesses

### Takeaway
TYMTR wraps phonics in a customisable-avatar narrative (build your own monster, earn accessories). Duolingo ABC uses a short, bite-sized path of varied micro-games with tracing and speech. Both rely on mild extrinsic rewards. The documented weaknesses are rigid starting points and little support for strugglers (Duolingo), paid mobile versions (TYMTR, $4.99 at review time), and evidence claims that run ahead of study designs (Reading Eggs, Nessy).

### Cited Findings
- [PD] TYMTR: design your monster (arms, legs, body, head, face, ears/horns); First Steps has an island crash-landing story and four activities per island; prizes are accessories and fruit (Game 1) and stars exchanged for accessories (Games 2–3); narration by Simon Farnaby; mobile $4.99, free computer version — [Horn Book review](https://hbook.com/story/teach-your-monster-to-read-app-review)
- [PD] TYMTR off-screen rewards: posters, certificates, letters from the monster, eBook rewards — [TYMTR classroom page](https://teachyourmonster.org/teachers-area/using-the-games-in-the-classroom)
- [PD] Duolingo ABC: tap, drag, listen, speak; tap-through stories, bubble popping, tracing letters, sound-to-letter matching; per-letter games covering tracing, sound, initial-sound words, upper/lower case and saying words aloud; speech recognition (needs mic permission); free; bite-size; "may be best in small doses"; iOS listed — [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/app/duolingo-abc-learn-to-read)
- [PD] Lexia Core5 and Amira are adaptive and assessment-heavy (see section 5); Amira is a 1:1 AI tutor that listens to oral reading — [Evidence for ESSA: Amira](https://evidenceforessa.org/?p=665)

### Inferences
- [INF] Borrowable features: (a) avatar ownership and customisation as motivation (TYMTR); (b) short sessions on a visible path (Duolingo); (c) tracing tied to letter-sound introduction (Duolingo, Jolly's letter formation); (d) gesture/action mnemonics per sound (Jolly), which suit kinaesthetic classroom use with Japanese children; (e) the "Fred" sound-talk character (RWI); (f) speech-checked read-aloud (Duolingo, Amira), though ASR accuracy for Japanese-accented child speech is unverified. A browser app on Chromebooks also faces mic-permission friction.
- [INF] Weaknesses to avoid: multiple-choice-only checks that reward guessing; forced start at zero with no placement; levelled non-decodable texts; reward economies that dominate learning time (accessory shops); single-profile limits (problematic for shared school Chromebooks); reliance on native-speaker vocabulary.

### Gaps
- Independent UX/teacher reviews with dates for TYMTR (beyond Horn Book), Nessy and Reading Eggs were not gathered.
- Current (2026) pricing and platform status of TYMTR mobile and Duolingo ABC Android/web availability: [UNK].

## 5. Independent evaluations vs company/marketing claims

### Takeaway
Of the programmes studied, only Read Write Inc. has a large independent RCT (EEF). It found a small, cautious effect: +1 month overall, +3 months for FSM pupils. Amira has an Evidence for ESSA "Moderate" rating. UFLI has a third-party (WestEd) quasi-experimental pilot. Reading Eggs and Lexia evidence is company-commissioned or correlational, and Nessy has only case studies. No independent efficacy study of TYMTR or Duolingo ABC was found, and Duolingo's published efficacy research covers its language courses, not ABC.

### Cited Findings
- [PD] **RWI (EEF, independent)**: effectiveness RCT of 131 disadvantaged primary schools (66 vs 65; control mostly used other phonics programmes), starting in 2016, evaluated by American Institutes for Research. Result: about +1 month reading progress; FSM pupils about +3 months (less secure). Many pupils were excluded from the analysis, so the main result should be interpreted cautiously. The sister programme Fresh Start (Y5–8) showed −2 months, with poor implementation (35% of schools delivered none) — [EEF news](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/eef-publishes-independent-evaluation-of-read-write-inc-phonics-and-fresh-start); [EEF project page](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/read-write-inc-and-fresh-start); [Full report PDF](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/documents/projects/Read-Write-Inc-and-Fresh-Start-Final.pdf). Effect size and padlock rating were not on the news page: [UNK] here.
- [PD] **UFLI (third-party evaluator)**: WestEd districtwide pilot (author Nicholas A. Gage), K–1, compared with non-UFLI students; "significant and meaningful" impact, larger with high fidelity. Effect sizes were not on the summary page — [WestEd summary](https://www.wested.org/resources/districtwide-pilot-study-of-ufli-foundations/); [full PDF on UFLI site](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Districtwide-Pilot-Study-of-UFLI-Foundations_FINAL.pdf). UFLI's own site claims "significant gains in phonemic awareness, decoding, and oral reading fluency" [CO] — [UFLI](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations). EdReports has reviewed UFLI (2025) — [EdReports UFLI K–2](https://edreports.org/reports/detail/ufli-ela-k-2-2025/kindergarten/gateway-one) (ratings not fetched).
- [PD] **Duolingo ABC**: Duolingo's efficacy roundup (published Sept 2024, modified May 2026) lists studies of its language courses (Spanish, French, English, conversation) and none of ABC — [Duolingo blog: efficacy results](https://blog.duolingo.com/results-duolingo-efficacy-studies). No ABC efficacy study found: [UNK]. Any ABC claims would be [CO].
- [PD] **TYMTR**: the developer cites teacher case studies and a Roehampton article on curriculum fit, not an outcome trial — [TYMTR classroom page](https://teachyourmonster.org/teachers-area/using-the-games-in-the-classroom). Searches found no peer-reviewed RCT of TYMTR. Note: a 2020 *Frontiers in Education* RCT that comes up in TYMTR searches is about **GraphoGame Rime**, not TYMTR. Its full RCT found GraphoGame roughly equivalent to business-as-usual for poor readers in Year 2; only exploratory subgroup gains appeared (g ≈ 0.33–0.68) — [Ahmed et al. 2020, Frontiers in Education](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feduc.2020.00132). This is a useful caution that phonics games alone may not beat ordinary teaching.
- [PD] **Reading Eggs [CO]**: claims ESSA Level III based on a correlational LearnPlatform study (~2,000 K–2 students; 51–121 lessons vs 0–50; NWEA MAP; significant in K and G1; no effect sizes; report filename dated May 2023). Correlational usage-vs-score data cannot show causation — [Reading Eggs ESSA article](https://readingeggs.com/articles/reading-eggs-meets-essa-rating-evidence-standards/). A 2017 company-hosted "93% improve" study is marketing, not evidence — [PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/2017/11/prweb14932201.htm).
- [PD] **Nessy [CO]**: four case studies (a correlation of its own assessment with SORT-R3; Godley School TX, n=152 K–1; two classrooms 2014–16, n=22; a teacher-run Glasgow pilot). No control groups, effect sizes or outcome data reported — [Nessy research page](https://www.nessy.com/en-gb/about-us/our-expert-research)
- [PD] **Lexia Core5 [CO-reported]**: claims ESSA Strong with a SAGE journal study (DOI 10.1177/00222194221141093), effect sizes "up to 0.53", and a mentioned "independent RAND evaluation". No WWC review is mentioned on the page — [Lexia ESSA evidence](https://www.lexialearning.com/research/essa-evidence)
- [PD] **Amira (independent rating)**: Evidence for ESSA "Moderate", 2 studies, n=15,602, mean ES +0.15. A student-randomised trial (n=178, grades 1–4 strugglers, vs sustained silent reading) gave +0.64. A matched quasi-experiment in Texas 2024–25 (n=15,424 K–1) gave +0.26 in K and +0.06 in G1 on DIBELS — [Evidence for ESSA: Amira](https://evidenceforessa.org/?p=665)

### Inferences
- [INF] The evidence base favours *systematic programme content plus routine* (RWI, UFLI) over any particular app. Apps with the most game-like design (TYMTR, Duolingo ABC, Reading Eggs) have the weakest independent outcome evidence. The new app should position itself as practice that supports teacher-led phonics, and build in its own simple pre/post measures (e.g. nonword reading, timed decodable word lists).
- [INF] Amira's large drop from K (+0.26) to G1 (+0.06) and GraphoGame's null overall result both suggest software effects are modest and context-dependent.

### Gaps
- EEF RWI effect size (Hedges' g) and padlock rating: in the full report, not extracted.
- No independent study of TYMTR or Duolingo ABC found. WWC reviews of any of these were not found in this pass.
- No EFL / Japanese-learner evaluation of any programme was found.

### Inferences (design synthesis for the new app)
- [INF] Borrow: UFLI's 8-step lesson skeleton; TYMTR's three-stage arc (letters+CVC → digraphs+sentences → alt spellings+books) and avatar ownership; RWI's one-spelling-first rule, Fred-style sound talk and three-reads routine; Jolly's actions; Duolingo's short sessions, visible path, tracing and speech read-aloud.
- [INF] Avoid: guess-friendly 2–4-option recognition as the only mastery check; no placement test; non-decodable "levelled" stories during the phonics phase; sight-word-first lessons; heavy currency/shop loops; single-profile designs on shared Chromebooks.
- [INF] EFL-specific additions none of the competitors offer: L1 (Japanese) support for instructions; contrast drills for sounds Japanese lacks or merges (/l/–/r/, /b/–/v/, /s/–/θ/, /f/–/h/, short vowels /æ/ /ʌ/ /ɑ/); warnings against epenthetic vowels in sound-talk (pure "s" not "su"); decodable vocabulary chosen from words the child already knows orally.
